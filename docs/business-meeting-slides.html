At this distributor, every order arrives by email, as a PDF or in the email text. Each customer writes it differently, with their own references. Someone types it into the ERP, Simax. Product choices, and why

Simple, safe, and it learns each customer

AI reads, rules match

The AI only reads the order. Fixed rules match it to the catalogue. Why: no invented references.

Flag, never guess

Doubtful lines are shown to a person, who fixes them in one click. Why: a wrong delivery costs more than a check.

Learns by memory

Each fix is saved for that customer, so their next order goes through alone. No model training.

A file, not an integration

Ordo outputs a file the ERP imports, plus a draft reply. Why: no IT project to start.

Four choices. One: the AI never picks a reference, it only copies what the customer wrote; matching is deterministic. Two: when in doubt, Ordo stops and asks a human. Three: corrections become per-customer aliases, so it gets better without retraining. Four: we export an import file, so a small distributor can start without an integration project.

Competition

Others serve big companies. We start small.

Enterprise tools

Esker, Conexiom, Comena, Proton.ai. Conexiom says it is “built for mid-market and enterprise”.

French players

Order1, Lisy.co, Deestree. Order1 targets SMEs and mid-caps, with no public price.

Today’s habits

Manual typing, EDI, customer portals. EDI and portals need the customer to change how they order.

Our gap to test: small distributors, a self-serve start, and memory per customer.

The market is not empty. But the established tools sell to mid-market and enterprise, with long sales cycles. Our bet: small distributors who need something they can start alone, cheaply, without changing how their own customers order.
